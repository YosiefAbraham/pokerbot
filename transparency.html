Bot transparency: how Yosief decides

Yosief (the Tight Profiler) is our no-limit hold'em bot. This page lays out every rule, formula and threshold it uses to choose an action, in the order it applies them, and why each one is there.

Values with a dotted underline and every hand grid are read live from the bot's code, so this page always matches the version that's running.

Play tightThe base range is any pocket pair plus AK–AT: — of 1,326 starting combos. Yosief opens up only when the table invites it.
Exploit, don't solveAgainst simple bots, punishing their habits beats game-theory-optimal play, which isn't practical to compute for 8-handed no-limit anyway.
Profile everyone, alwaysYosief tracks how often every opponent plays, raises, folds and bluffs, and whether they tilt. It keeps updating, so it notices when a bot changes gears.
Never crash, never act illegallyEvery action passes through one legality gate. Any error falls back to check or fold. A timeout costs more than any exploit gains.

decide() keeps no hidden state: the same situation and the same profiles always give the same action, even the simulation step, which is seeded from the cards. That makes every rule testable on a hand-built situation, and lets us replay any hand and get the same decision.

Ranking starting hands

Several ranges are "the top X% of hands". This is how hands get ranked.

Yosief scores all 169 starting hands with the Chen formula, a quick, simple strength estimate:

  1. Score the high card: A = 10, K = 8, Q = 7, J = 6, otherwise half its rank (T = 5, 9 = 4.5, … 2 = 1).
  2. Pairs: double it, minimum 5 (so 22 = 5, AA = 20). Done.
  3. Suited: add 2.
  4. Gap between the cards: subtract 0, 1, 2, 4 or 5 for a gap of 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4+ ranks.
  5. Connected low cards: add 1 if the gap is 0 or 1 and both cards are below a queen.

Hands are sorted by score and weighted by their combos (6 for a pair, 4 suited, 12 offsuit), giving each hand a percentile: the share of all 1,326 combos at least as strong. top(p) is every hand whose percentile is at most p.

Preflop

Yosief's seat, who has acted, and who is still to act decide which range applies.

Seats

Eight-handed, the seats in order of action are UTG, UTG1, MP, HJ, CO, BTN, SB, BB. They group into early (UTG, UTG1), middle (MP, HJ), late (CO, BTN), SB and BB. At smaller tables the earliest seats disappear first.

Nobody has raised yet (unopened or limped pot)

Yosief checks these in order; the first one that matches decides.

  1. Early seats: open the wider early range only when a calling station is still to act and a maniac isn't next to act; otherwise the tight early range. If nobody has limped, no maniac is next, and every opponent still to act folds to preflop raises more than — of the time, open the top 15% (minus small pairs). Middle and later seats: open the seat's range.
  2. Folded to the CO or button: raise to steal the blinds: top 27% from the CO, top 35% from the button.
  3. Small pairs (22–77): limp to set-mine, from any seat.
  4. A loose player limped: raise to isolate them with the top 27% of hands.
  5. Folded to the small blind: steal with STEAL, or STEAL_WIDE when the big blind folds to raises more than 65% of the time. Never steal when a calling station is in the big blind.
  6. Otherwise: check if it's free, else fold.

Raise size

size = bb × (4 + limpers + loose limpers), capped so the raise adds at most —× the pot Bigger against more limpers, and bigger still against loose ones, who call anyway. In an unraised pot the blinds total 1.5 bb, so the cap makes a standard open 3 bb.

Facing a raise

  1. Small pairs (22–77), any number of raises: call to set-mine only if the effective stack (see safety rails) can pay it off: call × — ≤ effective stack A set flops about 1 time in 8.5, so the call has to be small next to what's left to win.
  2. A loose raiser makes it more than — bb: a stack-off decision. Call with hands that beat junk (STACK_OFF_VS_LOOSE), fold the rest.
  3. One raise: pick the ranges by who raised, below. A re-raise is 3× the bet (3.5× from the blinds). Pairs not in the ranges can still set-mine by the formula above. In the big blind, also call a raise of 2.5 bb or less with BB_DEFEND.
  4. A re-raise: shove the four-bet range (a non-premium shove is held to the raise cap), call the call range if the call is at most 35% of the effective stack, fold the rest.

"Loose raiser" means one labeled maniac or loose, or one currently tilting (see profiles).

Postflop

After the flop, Yosief first sorts its hand into one of five buckets, counting only what its own cards add to the board.

BucketWhat's in it
monsterA straight or better, a set (pocket pair plus one on the board), or two pair using both hole cards including top pair
strongAn overpair, top pair with a ten-or-better kicker, trips using one hole card, or two pair without top pair
mediumAny other pair (underpair, middle pair, top pair with a weak kicker), or an overpair on a paired board
drawOn the flop or turn: four to a flush, or an open-ended straight draw (two different cards complete it, 8 outs)
airEverything else, including hands where the board plays

Small pairs override everything

With 22–77, unless it made a set or better (the monster bucket), Yosief checks and folds to any bet on every street, however small. They were in the hand to hit a set; without one they put in no more chips.

bet = committed + max(1 bb, fraction × pot) Bluffing is allowed only with no calling station in the pot, at most two opponents, and the least-folding of them folding to bets more than 55% of the time on this street (65% on the river).

Facing a bet

  1. Monster: raise to 3 × (committed + to call).
  2. A tight bettor's big late bet: fold if the bettor is a nit (or their bet sizing has told on them as "strong") and the bet is at least 75% of the pot on the turn or river.
  3. Everything else is an equity decision: call only if Yosief's estimated share of the pot beats the price.
price = to callpot + to call    call if   equity ≥ price + margin pot already includes the bet. A pot-sized bet costs 33%; a half-pot bet 25%. margin is 0 against a bluffy bettor and — otherwise. A bettor is bluffy if it's a maniac, bluffs the river more than 35% of the time, is tilting, or its bet size has told on it as "weak".

This is the "do my outs justify the call?" test done with arithmetic. The equity number counts Yosief's outs and every card that improves the opponents, as described next.

Equity simulation

How Yosief estimates its chance of winning a hand it can't see the end of.

  1. Give each opponent a range. An opponent who plays a share v of hands (their profiled vpip) holds a hand from top(max(v, —)). A bot that plays 70% gets nearly any two cards; a nit gets only premiums.
  2. Deal. Draw each opponent's cards at random from their range (up to 40 tries, then any two cards if the board has made the range impossible), then deal the rest of the board.
  3. Score the showdown. A win counts 1, a tie with k players counts 1/k, a loss 0.
  4. Repeat — times and average.
equity = wins + Σ 1/ktiesN With N = —, the estimate's standard error is about ±3 points. The random draws are seeded from Yosief's cards and the board, so the same spot always gives the same answer.
Example. 55 on a 7♥ 3♦ 7♣ flop: against one loose bot, equity is about 65%, so Yosief calls. Against four opponents it's about 22%, because every 7 or 3 out there beats it, so a 37% price is a fold.

A fast evaluator makes this affordable. It scores a seven-card hand in about 10 µs, checking each category directly instead of trying all 21 five-card combinations, and it matched the slower version on 300,000 random hands.

Labels

After — hands, each opponent gets the first label that fits:

  1. nit: plays under 12% of hands.
  2. maniac: aggression over 45% and plays over 35%.
  3. station: plays over 40% and folds to bets under 35%.
  4. folder: folds to bets over 60%.
  5. loose: plays over —.
  6. regular: everything else.

Many rules ask whether a player is loose: a maniac, a station, labeled loose, or currently tilting.

Tilt is measured, not assumed

tilting = within — hands of a ±— bb swing and vpipafter swing − vpip > — Bots only tilt if someone coded them to. Yosief flags tilt only for a player whose play has actually loosened after big swings before.

Bet-sizing tells

Once a player has shown down at least 3 strong and 3 weak hands after betting, and their average bet sizes for the two differ by at least 25% of the pot, Yosief reads a new bet as "strong" or "weak", whichever average it's closer to.

Safety rails

Rules that hold no matter what the strategy wants.

Stacks and the effective stack

Stacks carry over from hand to hand. A bot that busts buys back in for 100 big blinds, and winners' stacks keep growing with no cap (a "reset to 100bb every hand" mode also exists). So stacks at a table are usually uneven, and Yosief's own stack is the wrong yardstick. What matters is how much can actually be won or lost:

effective = min(our stack, deepest opponent still in the hand) Measured as totals for this street (chips already in plus chips behind). Set-mining odds, the 35% re-raise call, raise sizes and the all-in rule all use it. If a dealer doesn't report other players' stacks, Yosief falls back to its own.

Legality gate

  • Raises are clamped between the minimum legal raise and the cap, and never past the effective stack, since nobody could call more. If no legal raise fits, Yosief calls (or checks).
  • Yosief never folds when checking is free; a call or fold with nothing to call becomes a check.
  • Every public method is wrapped in error handling. If deciding fails, Yosief checks, or folds if checking isn't allowed.

Raise cap

raise to ≤ committed + — × pot No single raise adds more than —× the pot, even with aces. The only exception is a re-raise (a preflop 3-bet or more, or a postflop raise of a raise) with one of these hands. A raise that would leave less than 30% behind becomes an all-in only when it's uncapped.

All-in rule (after the flop)

Before any action that commits the effective stack (going all-in by raising or calling, or calling a shorter stack's all-in), Yosief checks its made hand, counting only what its own cards add:

  1. Three of a kind or better (a set, trips, straight, flush, full house): all-in allowed.
  2. One or two pair: allowed only if every opponent still in the hand is loose, because their ranges are wide.
  3. The board plays (Yosief's cards add nothing): never.

If the all-in isn't allowed, Yosief calls or checks instead, or folds if the call itself would be all-in.

Known limits

  • Bets and raises still use the five buckets, not equity, so they don't see board texture. A set on a four-flush board is still a "monster".
  • Opponent ranges come from preflop habits only. A player who bets the turn gets the same range as one who checks.
  • No multiway adjustment when betting first. Top pair is "strong" against one opponent or four.
  • Only the local dealer is wired up. Playing through a different dealer program needs only adapter.py filled in for its message format.
  • Thinking time: the equity simulation takes most decisions to 4–20 ms, with rare cases near 40 ms. If decisions need to be faster, fewer samples fix it.
